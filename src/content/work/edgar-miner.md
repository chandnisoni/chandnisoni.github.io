Every public company in America has been telling its own story to the SEC since the 1990s. Who runs it, who owns it, what it earned, what it is afraid of, who it signed with. Millions of filings. The answers to most questions an investor asks are in there, and almost nobody reads them, because they were written to be filed, not read.

Edgar Miner is that archive, rebuilt so it can be asked a question. Every filing since 1995, turned into facts and relationships, with an answer to "what was true on this date" and not just what is true now. For a person it looks like a conversation: ask about a company, get an answer with the filing it came from. For an AI agent it looks like a source it can trust, because every number points back to the page it came from.

The record that costs a fund tens of thousands a year today should be something a solo analyst, a founder, or an agent can use on a Tuesday morning. That is the whole idea.
